# Study percussion ensembles

Six scene studies in Grooves: First Ensemble, Son Marcha 2–3, and Bossa Nova are free; Three Surdos, Son Marcha 3–2, and Bembé are Pro. Every part stays visible in Split View;
use Solo or the Study Sequencer to hear its role. These are quantized teaching
arrangements, not claims to reproduce the feel or orchestration of every regional
tradition. No recorded performance or commercial sample is included.

## Sources and transcription decisions

### Brazilian samba (two scenes)

[Inspire-works: Brazilian Samba Drumming, Facilitator Lessons 1–4](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/587a2214bf629abac0867dc8/t/58ad63066a4963ab2fa9f270/1487758091007/Samba%2BFacilitator%2BLesson%2BPlans%2B1-4.pdf).
Read from the notation on PDF pages 2 and 6. The source explicitly describes a
samba-style classroom piece. These are **classroom ensemble arrangements**, not
full Rio bateria or Salvador samba-reggae transcriptions.

One-based sixteenth positions, one 4/4 teaching bar:

- Surdo 1 low: 5,13; surdo 2 high: 1,9.
- Caixa/repinique teaching rhythm: 1,4,5,7,9,11,13.
- Ganza: all 16, accenting 3,7,11,15.
- Tamborim teaching rhythm: 1,3,4,7,8,9,11,12,15,16.
- Lesson 3 third surdo: 1,5,6,9,13,14,15,16.

Kick and tuned toms approximate the surdo pitch hierarchy; snare approximates
caixa; shaker approximates ganza; wood block supplies a short tamborim proxy.
The layer labels describe ensemble roles, not the actual synthesized instruments.

### Cuban son practice arrangements (2–3 and 3–2)

[Berklee PULSE: Create Your Own Afro-Latin Groove](https://pulse.berklee.edu/content/public/pub_unit_assets/lessons/lesson6_banded/BANDED_Lesson_6_Create_Your_Own_Groove.pdf)
provides the 2–3 son-clave/cascara teaching foundation used by the existing Son
Clave Groove scene. [Kevin Zahner: Basic Conga Techniques](https://rhythmnotes.net/basic-conga-techniques/)
describes the basic marcha with slap on beat 2 and open tones on beat 4 and its
and. This implementation separates open, slap and quiet touch into three layers,
then combines them with shell, clave and eighth-note shaker as an **authored
educational son-style arrangement**, not a transcription of a traditional group.

32 sixteenths = two bars, eight quarter-note beats. Open tones: 13,15,29,31;
slaps: 5,21; touches: 1,3,7,9,11 and those positions +16. Son clave: 5,9,17,23,29.
The opposite-direction scene rotates **every part** by 16 positions; it does not
reverse or change the clave type. Tom/snare are explicit conga sound substitutes;
the engine cannot reproduce heel/toe technique or genuine conga open/slap timbres.

### Bembe timeline and cross-rhythm

[Sunhouse: The Rhythmic Worlds of Bembe](https://sunhou.se/blog/the-rhythmic-worlds-of-bembe)
identifies a 12-pulse timeline with seven attacks and spacing 2-2-1-2-2-2-1.
This gives positions 1,3,5,6,8,10,12. The four-pulse and six-pulse drums plus
shaker are an **authored counting study**, not traditional supporting drum parts.
The four controls called quarter-note beats in the app represent four dotted-quarter
pulses in this compound-meter interpretation. Do not market this scene as a full
religious/ceremonial Bembe ensemble.

### Bossa nova percussion

[Mark Kelso / Drumeo: Introduction to Brazilian Grooves, The Bossa Nova](https://drumeoblog.s3.amazonaws.com/Resources/dci-70%20%281%29.pdf),
page 1, basic cross-stick and bass-drum patterns. Cross-stick on 32 sixteenths:
1,7,13,21,27. Kick: 1,7,9,15 repeated +16. Hats: odd positions (eighths).
This is a **drum-set adaptation**, not a full Brazilian percussion ensemble.
Wood Block substitutes for cross-stick; the cross-stick figure is not called son clave.

## App behavior

All six scenes are dry, unlinked, share a real editable grid, and start with all
parts audible. They open in Split View. Tempo is a moderate teaching choice,
not a claimed canonical performance tempo. Source patterns remain straight-grid:
no artificial swing or randomized timing has been applied. Layer roles appear
under L1/L2/etc. and in Split View exports. Save/load preserves those labels.
